# Spatial Identity

## Urban Character

Tecate's target identity is a border-region urban fabric with commercial density, residential adjacency, irregular construction, visible terrain, and recognizable local landmarks.

The simulation should feel lived-in and regionally specific without relying on photorealistic excess.

## Corridor Pattern

The first iteration focuses on four corridors:

- boulevard Juarez;
- avenida Miguel Hidalgo;
- avenida Revolucion;
- avenida Nuevo Leon.

The corridors should preserve road continuity, intersection legibility, approximate scale, and the relationship between commercial frontage and nearby residential fabric.

## Materials

Dominant exterior material families:

- painted plaster and stucco;
- concrete;
- masonry;
- metal security grilles;
- corrugated metal;
- ceramic tile accents;
- asphalt;
- sun-faded signage panels;
- painted curb markings.

Materials should show regional dryness, dust, sun exposure, and commercial repainting without becoming exaggerated decay.

## Signage

Signage should communicate regional commercial density:

- painted shop signs;
- wall-mounted panels;
- vertical signs;
- hand-painted service labels;
- small awnings;
- mixed typography.

Text-bearing signs require manual or curated review when they represent identifiable businesses or historic claims.

## Vegetation

Vegetation should reflect an urban dry-region mix:

- street trees where documented or plausible;
- sparse planted areas;
- dry grasses and shrubs in low-maintenance zones;
- ornamental vegetation near commercial or residential fronts;
- regional terrain vegetation outside the dense urban core.

Vegetation scatter must not obscure critical road geometry, entrances, landmarks, or mountain sightlines.

## Terrain And Horizon

Light terrain variation is central to the Tecate feel. Streets should not be flattened into a generic grid.

Montaña Cuchumá must remain geospatially coherent. It should participate in lighting, fog, and atmospheric perspective as distant terrain. Its silhouette recognition is more important than fine terrain microdetail.

## Density

The visual density goal is regional and pedestrian-scale:

- frequent storefront rhythm;
- varied building heights;
- small setbacks and irregular edges;
- service alleys and side conditions where data supports them;
- utility infrastructure;
- low walls and fences;
- signs, awnings, doors, and shutters.

Density should not produce navigational clutter or hide the corridor structure.

## Lighting

The baseline lighting direction should support:

- dry daylight clarity;
- readable building fronts;
- visible terrain relief;
- atmospheric separation for distant mountains;
- future time-of-day variation.

The first iteration may use simple lighting, but it must not conflict with the eventual need for distant terrain, fog, and interior transitions.

## Perceptual Tests

A corridor pass should ask:

- Can the player read the road direction without a map?
- Does the scale feel walkable?
- Are terrain changes visible?
- Does the horizon remain coherent?
- Does the scene avoid generic suburb cues?
- Are landmarks and major corridors protected from procedural distortion?
